# Volume & Velocity

*Source: extracted from `the-edge-wiki/Frameworks/Volume & Velocity.md`.*

## The Principle
Creative production targets are an *output* of four variables — Win Rate, Half-Life, Churn Rate, Scenario Planning — not an arbitrary monthly quota.

## In Plain English
Most agencies set "produce X ads per month" as a vibe. The right number is mathematical: how often do your ads work, how fast do winners die, how much of your winner pool churns each month, and what's coming on the calendar. Multiply through and you know exactly how many new distinct assets you need.

## The Four Variables

### 1. Win Rate
What % of ads launched become profitable winners? If 1 in 10 ads wins, you need 10x the production of a 1-in-2 account.

### 2. Half-Life
How many days until 50% of your winners fatigue? Short half-life = more constant production pressure. Longer half-life = headroom.

### 3. Churn Rate
What % of your winner pool dies each month? Sets the replacement rate.

### 4. Scenario Planning
Scaling pushes, seasonal peaks, new product launches — the multipliers on baseline production targets.

## How To Apply
1. Measure historical Win Rate (% of launched ads that became profitable)
2. Measure Half-Life (days until 50% of winners fatigue)
3. Measure monthly Churn Rate of the winner pool
4. Layer Scenario Planning multipliers — scaling pushes, seasonal peaks, launches
5. Multiply through for a concrete monthly production target
6. Allocate the target across the Persona x Format Grid — never produce volume without diversity
7. Re-measure quarterly — Win Rate and Half-Life shift as the account matures

## Anti-Patterns
- Setting a flat "X ads/month" target without measuring win rate or half-life
- Reactive production when winners die instead of structural graduation pipeline
- Producing volume without diversity (fails Squint Test; collapses Entity ID count)
- Using last quarter's Win Rate forever — the metric drifts as the account matures
