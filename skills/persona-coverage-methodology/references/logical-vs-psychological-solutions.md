# Logical vs Psychological Solutions

*Source: extracted from `the-edge-wiki/Concepts/Logical vs Psychological Solutions.md`.*

## What It Is
Most people solve problems with logical solutions. But logical solutions have usually been tried — because they're logical. What's left are psychological solutions: changing perception rather than changing reality.

## Why It Matters
"Any fool can sell a product by offering it for a discount. It takes great marketing to sell the same product for a premium." (Rory Sutherland) The psychological solution is often cheaper AND more effective than the logical one.

## Key Facts
- Logical solutions tend to be already tried because they are obvious; psychological solutions remain because they require reframing perception
- Psychological solutions are usually **cheaper** to implement than logical ones
- Psychological solutions often produce **bigger** outcome changes than equivalent logical ones (e.g., the train map vs. faster trains)
- The Value Equation is fundamentally about perception — perceived likelihood and perceived time delay drive choice, not actual values

## Examples
- **Trains** (Rory Sutherland): Logical = make trains faster (billions). Psychological = add a dotted map showing when the next train arrives (millions). Result: bigger satisfaction increase from the map.
- **Elevators**: Logical = make elevator faster. Psychological = add floor-to-ceiling mirrors so people stare at themselves and forget they're waiting.
- **Products**: Logical = make it cheaper. Psychological = make fewer of them and raise the price, which causes people to want it more.

## Application to gap concept briefs
When sketching a concept for a gap micro-persona, look for the psychological reframe — the angle that changes how the prospect *perceives* the product or their problem, not just what the product *is*. The strongest concepts often re-name the problem entirely.
