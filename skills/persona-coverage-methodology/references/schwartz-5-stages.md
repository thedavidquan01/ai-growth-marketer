# Eugene Schwartz — 5 Stages of Awareness

*Authored reference for the persona-coverage plugin (the source vault doesn't have a dedicated page for this framework).*

## What it is
Eugene Schwartz's taxonomy of where a prospect sits relative to a product, from his 1966 book *Breakthrough Advertising*. The headline and message angle that converts a Most Aware buyer is wrong — actively wrong, not just inefficient — for an Unaware prospect. Matching message to awareness stage is one of the highest-leverage moves in direct-response marketing.

## Why it's the right lens for persona coverage
Most ad portfolios over-index on **Product Aware** and **Most Aware** prospects (the people already searching for the brand or category). They under-serve **Problem Aware** and **Unaware** prospects, which is where the bigger TAM and the lower CPMs live. Tagging both portfolio ads and gap candidates by awareness stage surfaces this skew quickly.

## The 5 stages (least to most aware)

### Stage 1 — Unaware
The prospect doesn't know they have the problem. They have symptoms but no name for them. They're not searching for solutions because they don't know they need one.

**Message angle that works**: name the problem they're living with. Story-led, identity-led, "wait — is this me?" hooks. Educational and pattern-naming.

**Example hook**: "Most women in their late 30s blame this on stress. It's not stress."

**Wrong angles**: anything starting with the product name, anything that assumes the prospect knows the category exists.

### Stage 2 — Problem Aware
The prospect knows they have a problem. They don't know solutions exist, or they assume the existing solutions won't work for them.

**Message angle that works**: validate the problem ("you're not crazy, here's why this happens"), then introduce the existence of a solution category. Pain articulation lands hardest here.

**Example hook**: "If you've tried every diet and still can't lose the last 10 pounds, your hormones might be the issue."

**Wrong angles**: feature comparisons, specific product benefits, "best X for Y" framing.

### Stage 3 — Solution Aware
The prospect knows solutions exist. They don't know your solution exists, or they're evaluating categories.

**Message angle that works**: differentiate the *type* of solution. "Why pills don't work and what does." Mechanism-of-action framing. Category-creation moves.

**Example hook**: "Why your supplement isn't working — and the one nutrient missing from 90% of them."

**Wrong angles**: pure brand storytelling (they don't know you yet), or assuming they need education on the problem (they're past that).

### Stage 4 — Product Aware
The prospect knows your product exists. They're comparing it to alternatives, looking for proof, working through objections.

**Message angle that works**: testimonials, specific results, objection handling, social proof, before/after, founder story, third-party validation.

**Example hook**: "I tried 4 hormonal supplements before this one. Here's what's different."

**Wrong angles**: "what is the problem" framing — they're past it.

### Stage 5 — Most Aware
The prospect knows your product, knows it works, and just needs a reason to buy *now*. Existing customers, retargeting audiences, brand searchers.

**Message angle that works**: offer-stack, urgency, scarcity, specific deal, restock reminder, bundle, price.

**Example hook**: "20% off ends Friday. Restock before [season]."

**Wrong angles**: anything that re-explains the brand or product — they know.

## How to use this reference

### When tagging portfolio ads (Phase 1)
For each ad, ask: what does this hook *assume* the viewer already knows? If it assumes nothing → Unaware. If it assumes knowledge of the problem → Problem Aware. Etc. Then tally the distribution. Most brands are 70%+ in Stages 4–5 and have abandoned 1–3.

### When mining Reddit (Phase 2)
For each candidate micro-persona, the verbatim quotes reveal the awareness stage. "I don't know why I feel this way" → Unaware. "I know my hormones are off but nothing's worked" → Problem Aware. "Should I try X or Y?" → Solution Aware. Tag accordingly so the synthesizer's concept brief matches the prospect's stage.

### When the strategist reviews
Reject any portfolio analysis that doesn't surface the awareness-stage distribution as a finding. Reject any gap candidate whose proposed message angle mismatches the prospect's stage (e.g. running a Most Aware "20% off" hook to an Unaware prospect — they don't know why they'd want the discount).

## The high-leverage gap most brands miss
The Stage 1–2 (Unaware → Problem Aware) territory is where the unmined audience pockets live. It's also where Reddit verbiage is gold — Reddit posts are usually written by Stage 1–2 prospects trying to *name* what they're feeling. Their language *is* the hook.
