# Steven Reiss — 16 Basic Desires

*Authored reference for the persona-coverage plugin (the source vault doesn't have a dedicated page for this framework).*

## What it is
Steven Reiss's empirically-derived taxonomy of 16 motivational desires that explain almost all goal-directed human behavior. Identified by analyzing what people across cultures said motivated them, then statistically reducing to the smallest set that explained the variance. Published in *Who Am I? The 16 Basic Desires That Motivate Our Actions and Define Our Personalities* (2000).

## Why it's the right lens for persona coverage
Demographics tell you *who* a prospect is. Reiss desires tell you *what they want to feel*. A coverage matrix built only on demographics misses that two people in the same demographic can be driven by entirely different desires — and an ad that hits one will leave the other cold. Tagging each ad and each gap-candidate by its dominant Reiss desire surfaces motivational gaps demographic analysis can't see.

## The 16 desires

For each: name, definition, what it looks like in ad copy / hooks.

1. **Power** — desire to influence others, to lead, to dominate. *Hook signal*: "Take control", "command the room", authority/expert framing.
2. **Independence** — desire for self-reliance, autonomy, freedom from external control. *Hook signal*: "Do it on your own terms", anti-establishment, no-permission framing.
3. **Curiosity** — desire to learn, to know, to understand. *Hook signal*: "What scientists discovered…", education-led, mystery-led hooks.
4. **Acceptance** — desire for approval, belonging, inclusion, freedom from rejection. *Hook signal*: "You're not alone", community framing, "we get you".
5. **Order** — desire for structure, predictability, organization, clean systems. *Hook signal*: "Finally, a system that…", checklist framing, clean before/after.
6. **Saving** — desire to collect, to keep, to not waste. *Hook signal*: "Don't throw away…", value-stacking, "what you're missing".
7. **Honor** — desire to live by a moral code, loyalty to one's group/values. *Hook signal*: "Stand for something", values-aligned framing, identity-as-code.
8. **Idealism** — desire for social justice, fairness, improving the world. *Hook signal*: "Change the system", impact framing, mission-led.
9. **Social Contact** — desire for friendship, companionship, peer interaction. *Hook signal*: "Bring your friends", group-experience framing.
10. **Family** — desire to raise one's own children, parental investment. *Hook signal*: "For your kids", legacy framing, parent-archetype talent.
11. **Status** — desire for prestige, social standing, being respected/admired. *Hook signal*: "What top performers do", visible markers, peer-comparison.
12. **Vengeance** — desire to get even, to compete, to win against rivals. *Hook signal*: "Prove them wrong", underdog-revenge framing, competitive.
13. **Romance** — desire for sex, beauty, aesthetic pleasure. *Hook signal*: "Be wanted", beauty/desirability framing, sensual aesthetics.
14. **Eating** — desire for food, taste, satiation. *Hook signal*: food porn, taste-led, sensory-language hooks.
15. **Physical Activity** — desire to exercise, move, exert the body. *Hook signal*: "Earn your…", performance framing, sweat-aesthetic.
16. **Tranquility** — desire to be safe, calm, to avoid anxiety/threat. *Hook signal*: "Finally relax", "stop worrying", calm-aesthetic, safety framing.

## How to use this reference

### When tagging portfolio ads (Phase 1)
Pick the **single best-fit** Reiss desire for each ad based on its dominant emotional pull. Most ads pull on one primarily even if a secondary is implied. After tagging the full portfolio, look at the distribution. Most brands cluster in 2–3 desires and have completely abandoned the other 13 — that's the gap.

### When mining Reddit (Phase 2)
For each candidate micro-persona, identify which Reiss desire the verbatim quotes are *about*. The quote "I just want my body back" maps to Romance + Status. "I want my doctor to actually listen" maps to Acceptance + Power. The mapping must be defensible from the quote itself, not imported from outside.

### When the strategist reviews
Reject any candidate whose Reiss tag doesn't follow from the quotes. Reject portfolios whose Reiss-distribution finding is "covers all 16" — no real brand does, and that conclusion means the tagging was loose.

## Common mis-applications
- Tagging by *product category* instead of *emotional pull* (a beauty product can be Romance, but it can also be Tranquility, Acceptance, or Status — the ad determines the tag, not the SKU)
- Using "Status" as a default catch-all (it's a deep universal driver, but not every ad pulls on it as the *primary* surface)
- Confusing Acceptance (belonging) with Honor (values-alignment) — Acceptance wants to fit in; Honor wants to stand for something even if it costs belonging
